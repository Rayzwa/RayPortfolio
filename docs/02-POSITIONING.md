## 1. Target roles

**Draft (confirm, see §8):** Associate PM / PM II roles in **AI products**, **fintech**, and **B2B data tools**.

Why it matters: the hero, the skills list and the case-study choice should all point at the same target. A site that tries to appeal to everyone persuades no one.

## 2. Positioning statement

**Template**
> For **[teams]** who **[need]**, Raymond is a **[category]** who **[differentiator]**, unlike **[alternative]**, because **[proof]**.

**Draft from the current bio**
> For teams turning messy, data-heavy problems into AI tools people actually use, Raymond is a product manager who builds the bridge from ambiguity to a working solution, and who earns trust by doing the work rather than claiming the title. He has gone from intern to full-time PM at JPMorgan Chase in a year, across risk processing, AI products and commercial real estate.

## 5. Voice & tone

- **Plain:** short sentences, no jargon a recruiter would trip on.
- **Specific:** "streamlined risk-processing intake" beats "drove impact".
- **Curious:** questions and learning are a feature, not a weakness.
- **Avoid:** passionate, synergy, leverage (as a verb), rockstar, "results-driven", unexplained acronyms.

### Before → after

| Where | Current | Suggested direction |
|---|---|---|
| Hero headline | "a product manager who turns ambiguity into shipped outcomes" | Keep the idea but show it: "I turn messy, data-heavy problems into AI tools people use." Only claim "shipped" once a case study shows it. |
| Impact intro | "instead of headline numbers, here's what I've actually spent my time on" | Good honesty. Add one concrete, disclosure-safe outcome per item (e.g. "cut a manual review step"). |
| Testimonial | "He has developed a voice and learns quickly." | Reads like review language. Replace with a named quote that tells a specific story. |

## 6. Proof gaps & plan to close them

| Gap | Why it hurts | Plan | Phase |
|---|---|---|---|
| No real case study | Hiring managers can't see product thinking | Write case study #1 (risk-processing internship), disclosure-safe | 1 |
| No named testimonials | Anonymous quotes read as unverifiable | Ask 2 colleagues (a manager plus an engineer/designer) for a 2-sentence named quote | 1 |
| No metrics | Impact section feels thin | Use directional or qualitative outcomes the disclosure review approves | 1 |
| No public artifact | Nothing a stranger can verify | One teardown of a public AI product | 3–5 |
| Skills list looks template-default | Invites interview questions you can't back up | Keep only skills you can discuss with an example | 0 |

## 7. Disclosure guardrails

Review with your manager or compliance before publishing anything about JPMorgan Chase work.

- [ ] No client names, deal names, internal system or product code names
- [ ] No non-public numbers (volumes, dollar amounts, headcount)
- [ ] No screenshots of internal tools
- [ ] Describe *problem type*, *your approach*, *decisions and tradeoffs*, *what you learned*
- [ ] Check the firm's social-media / outside-communications policy
- [ ] Get written OK if anything is borderline
